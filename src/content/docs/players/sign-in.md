---
title: Your account
description: Why you need a NodeMP account, how to create one and sign in to the launcher through your browser, how to play without one in Test Drive, and what a ban means.
---

You can play NodeMP with an account or without one. With an account you have one name on every server, and servers can check that it's really you. Without an account, in Test Drive, NodeMP gives you a name and you can't choose it; some servers don't let guests in.

## Create an account

Create an account on the website: [nodemp.com/register](https://nodemp.com/register). You need:

- a username: 3 to 24 characters, Latin letters, digits, hyphens and underscores;
- an e-mail address: not a disposable one, because the confirmation link goes there;
- a password: 8 to 200 characters, not on lists of leaked or common passwords;
- two checkboxes: *I accept the Terms of Use and the Privacy Policy* and *I consent to the processing of my personal data*.

The account appears once you open the link in the e-mail. You use the same account if you run a server of your own: there is no separate account for hosts. If you forget your password, click **Forgot your password?** on the sign-in page. The reset link arrives by e-mail and is valid for one hour.

## Sign in to the launcher

The launcher never asks for your password: you sign in on the website, in your browser.

1. In the sign-in window, click **Sign in with browser**. The launcher shows a sign-in code and opens nodemp.com.
2. Sign in on the site if you aren't signed in yet. If two-factor authentication is on for your account, the site asks for the code from your app.
3. Check that the code on the site matches the one in the launcher and click **Allow sign-in**. The launcher greets you and opens the main window.

The code is valid for 10 minutes. While the launcher waits, it shows the time left (*Waiting for approval · 9:59*). **Open again** opens the page once more, **Copy link** copies the link and the code, and **Back** returns to the choice.

The launcher remembers your sign-in, so next time it opens the main window at once.

### If sign-in doesn't work

| What it says | What to do |
|---|---|
| *The browser did not open. Go to the address below and enter this code.* | The link and the code are already on your clipboard. Open the address in any browser and enter the code. |
| *The code expired. Get a new one and confirm in the browser.* | Click **Get a new code**. |
| *Sign-in was declined in the browser.* | Someone clicked **Deny** on the site. If it was you, click **Get a new code**. |
| *Browser sign-in did not work: …* | The text after the colon is the reason or NodeMP's answer. If the connection breaks, the launcher repeats the request up to four times. Click **Get a new code**. If NodeMP names a support address, a **Support** button appears beside the message. |

## Test Drive

**Continue as Test Drive** lets you in without an account. A guest name looks like `Guest-7K3MQ` and doesn't change as long as you join from the same launcher installation.

Each server decides whether it lets guests in. In the list you can tell by the **Test Drive** mark and by the **Access** row in the server's card ([Join a server](/players/join/#find-a-server)). If a guest joins a server that needs an account, the launcher refuses and offers **Sign in**.

Test Drive isn't remembered: the sign-in window comes back the next time you start the launcher. To sign in later, click **Settings → Account → Sign in**. A guest doesn't join the queue of a full server ([When the server is full](/players/join/#when-the-server-is-full)).

## Your account in the launcher

**Settings → Account** shows your name and roles. **Sign out** switches the launcher to Test Drive. The **On the site** block has links to the account pages: **Profile**, **Security**, **Connections**, **My servers**, **Session** and **Public page**. They open in your browser.

If the moderators give you a warning, the launcher tells you and shows the reason under **Warnings from the moderators**. On the site, the warning shows on your profile while it is in force.

## Bans

A banned account can't join NodeMP servers. The launcher shows the *Account banned* window at start-up, when you sign in, or when you try to join a server. The window shows **Reason**, **Until** (or *No end date*) and the account number. If there are no details, it says *No reason or end date was given.*

**Close** closes the launcher. If NodeMP names a support address, a **Contact support** button appears beside it. A ban with an end date lifts by itself: after that date the launcher works as usual.

A ban on a single server is given by that server's host and doesn't affect other servers. The server tells you about it when you join: `You are banned from this server`, or the reason the host wrote.
